Two systems

Fast and slow, as an architecture question.

Systems · Constraints · Agents

Kahneman described two modes of human thinking. The useful move is to stop treating it as psychology and read it as a design for where work should go.

System 1 — fast

Automatic, parallel, always on. Answers without deliberating and reports how sure it is.

Cheap enough to run on everything. Handles the overwhelming majority of cases and never gets tired of them.

Its job is not to be brilliant. Its job is to be right often enough, and to know when it is not.

System 2 — slow

Deliberate, serial, effortful. Gathers evidence, weighs conflicting rules, reasons through a case it has not seen before.

Expensive, and strictly limited. In an organisation it is the senior buyer, the controller, the duty engineer — or a frontier model.

Its job is the small number of cases that genuinely need it.

The architectural error is not choosing the wrong system. It is routing everything to System 2 because it is the one you trust.

Keep the Kahneman reference light. Nobody needs the psychology lecture; they need the routing idea.

The closing line is the whole slide. Most organisations, and almost every first agent deployment, send every case to the expensive deliberative path because that is the path they trust. That is running your most constrained station flat out on work it should never have seen.

Ask the room: in your process, what fraction of cases genuinely need a senior person to think? The answer is usually under ten percent, and the system is built as though it were all of them.
