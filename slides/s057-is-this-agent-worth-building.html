II · ROI worksheet

Is this agent worth building?

Systems · Constraints · Agents

Add-on · slide A1

Three quantities. Estimate each before you write the first line of code.

ΔT

Change in Throughput

Additional value the system generates because the constraint moved.

•How many more units flow through the constraint per week?

•What does each unit unlock downstream? (revenue, avoided cost, time)

•Translate to currency, conservatively.

ΔOE

Change in Operating Expense

Ongoing cost to keep the agent running.

•Model and infra costs per month

•Human review and supervision time

•Monitoring, maintenance, retraining

ΔI

Change in Investment

One-time spend to build and integrate.

•Build, integration, change management

•Data preparation and connectors

•Pilot, evaluation, security review

Decision rule

Build if ΔT − ΔOE is positive and meaningfully larger than ΔI / payback period you can defend.

This is the worksheet teams should fill in BEFORE writing code. Most agent projects skip this step and discover too late that they were optimising a non-constraint or that ΔT was small relative to ΔI.

Two cautions to share verbally:

1) Estimate ΔT conservatively. If you cannot quantify it, you do not understand the system well enough yet.

2) Many teams forget the human-supervision cost in ΔOE. An agent that needs heavy review can have ΔOE close to the original cost.
