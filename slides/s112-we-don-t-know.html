We Don’t Know

Spend ninety seconds on this slide. It does important work — honors yesterday's session and positions today's correctly. Without it, the cohort will quietly wonder whether we are saying the same thing again, or worse, contradicting yesterday.

What to say, in your own voice:

Yesterday you learned to see customer processes as circuits — to spot resistance, mismatch, delay. That is a powerful diagnostic. It tells you where value is leaking.

Today we sharpen that view in one specific direction. In pipeline-shaped processes — and most customer business processes are pipeline-shaped — not every leak is equal. One station governs the throughput of the whole system; the others are noise, or sometimes worse, distraction. Acting on a leak that is not the constraint can actually make the system worse, by accelerating work into the queue behind the real constraint.

So yesterday found candidates. Today selects the one. And then asks: what kind of intervention belongs there? Sometimes an agent. Often something else. We will learn how to tell.

Optional vocabulary borrow during the workshop, lifted from PCD if useful: 'voltage' for strength of business intent, 'white box / black box' for what to open versus what to leave alone (maps directly to ToC's exploit / subordinate).
