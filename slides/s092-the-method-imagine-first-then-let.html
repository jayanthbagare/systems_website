Imagine at the frontier — unconstrained. That kick lifts the problem out of the basin it sits in today. Then watch where it settles under honest scrutiny. Where it lands is the altitude you commit to.

This is the spine of the whole block. The one place the dynamical-systems framing is fully load-bearing, not decorative.

You don't classify first and imagine second. You imagine at the frontier — unconstrained — and THAT is the perturbation. It lifts the problem out of whatever basin it currently sits in. Then you let it move and watch where it settles as the energy dissipates.

Deeper basins = more stable = automation (one answer, every time). Shallow basin = frontier (unstable, easily perturbed to somewhere new). Most problems, kicked hard, fall right back into automation. That's honest.

Note the automation basin is drawn deepest and the frontier shallowest — deliberately.
