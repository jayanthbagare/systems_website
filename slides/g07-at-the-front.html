Presenter's guide · Habits

What the speaker notes keep saying

Distilled from every note in the deck. If you read nothing else before presenting, read these.

In the labs

  • The PM presents. Walk the room looking for a PM who has gone quiet; that is when you step in.
  • Ask, don't correct. “What happens if this loop runs twice as fast?” “And then what happened?” — until they reach the vendor pool.
  • Pick pods for disagreement while walking, not by asking for volunteers. Early agreement means nobody looked hard enough.
  • Hold the line on exploit. Pods reach for hire, buy, automate because it feels ambitious. Step 2 is where the free throughput is.

At the front

  • Day 1 is diagnosis only. Every agent question goes on a flipchart for Day 2.
  • Never hint before a reveal. The value of the reveal is proportional to how committed they were to the wrong answer.
  • Say the number, say the sentence, stop talking. The quiet after #44 does more than a fourth bullet.
  • Reference slides are fifteen seconds. Notes that say “photograph” mean: let them, then move.
  • Read the coda once. Then nothing.

Most of what goes wrong in this workshop is the presenter filling a silence or rescuing a pod too early. The deck is built to let the room find things. Your job is mostly to protect that.
