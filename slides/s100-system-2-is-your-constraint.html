System 2 is your constraint.

Deliberation is the scarce resource in every process on the last slide — a senior buyer, or a frontier model. System 1 is how you subordinate to it.

2

Exploit — stop wasting deliberation.

Every case that reaches System 2 and did not need to is throughput taken from the constraint. Triage is not overhead. It is the exploit step.

3

Subordinate — the confidence threshold is the dial.

Escalate above it, decide below it. Move the threshold and you move the load on your scarcest resource, directly and measurably.

4

Elevate — only now.

Add deliberation capacity when triage is already tuned. Most teams buy this first and wonder why nothing improved.

That threshold is also the OE control. It sets how often you pay for the expensive path.

Your turn: name one step in your process where a confident, cheap, imperfect answer would be worth more than a slow correct one — and say what confidence you would require before letting it through.

This slide is why the block belongs in the workshop rather than being AI news. The five focusing steps apply to attention exactly as they apply to a machine station.

The subordination point is the sharp one. In a plant, subordinating means every other station runs at the pace the constraint absorbs. Here it means every case that can be resolved without deliberation must be, so the deliberative capacity is spent only on what needs it.

Two concrete harness uses worth mentioning if the room is technical: routing the request to the cheapest model that can handle it, and gating risky tool calls before they execute. The second one used to be locked inside closed coding harnesses; it is now something a pod can build. That is the checkpoint element from loop engineering, made cheap.

Close with the exercise and let pods answer it for their own process. Five minutes, one sentence each, and the required confidence stated as a number — make them commit to a figure, because that is the moment it becomes an engineering decision instead of a preference.
