V · The connection

The stopping condition is the OE control.

Systems · Constraints · Agents

Operating expense stopped being a parameter you estimate. It is a function of the loop you engineered.

A loop that converges

Terminates on a condition you chose. Bounded call count.

OE is estimable, defensible, and stays inside the number you took to your CFO.

A loop that circles

No stopping condition, or one that never fires on the hard cases.

OE has no upper bound — and the cheaper inference gets, the longer it takes anyone to notice.

You already have the lever. Stopping condition, checkpoints, verification — they are on the loop engineering slide.

Those are not only quality controls. They are the cost model.

This is the slide that earns the block. Day 1 taught T, OE and I as three quantities to estimate. Day 2 taught loop engineering as a reliability discipline. This joins them: the same four elements that make a loop trustworthy are what make its cost knowable.

For the systems-thinking pods: a converging loop sits in a basin. A circling loop has no attractor, so its cost term has no upper bound — and falling unit prices flatten the landscape further, which means the drift is slower to detect, not faster.
