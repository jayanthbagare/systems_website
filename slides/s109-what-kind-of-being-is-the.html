Coda · Ontological legitimacy

What kind of being is the agent

in your customer's system?

tool · actor · representative · simulator

Each is a different ontology.

Each carries a different legitimacy.

The throughput equation does not answer this. You do.

This is the slide you leave them with. Do not rush it. Do not explain it.

Read the question aloud, once, slowly. Then pause for at least five seconds before saying anything else.

If you say anything, say something close to this:

We spent today on the operational question. Find the constraint. Build at it. Prove the throughput. Those skills will serve you well in every customer engagement you have.

But there is a question underneath, that the throughput equation does not touch. When you deploy an agent into a customer's system, you are not just adding an automation. You are introducing something into their universe of actors — something that will sign documents, make decisions, escalate, defer, sometimes act when no human is watching. What kind of being is that?

If it is a tool — then it is your customer's instrument, and the responsibility for what it does rests entirely with them.

If it is an actor with standing — then it must be governed, certified, held accountable. New institutions will have to exist that do not exist today.

If it is a representative — then we must be very clear whose representative it is. Yours. Your customer's. The vendor of the model. The integrator who deployed it. Probably all four, in proportions that no contract today specifies.

If it is a simulator — then it is exploring possible worlds on behalf of the people who will live in those worlds, and the question is whether they have consented to the simulation.

Each of these is a different ontology. Each demands a different legitimacy. None of them are decided yet. They will be decided in the next five years, in rooms we may or may not be in.

I leave you with the question. Not the answer.

End the workshop here. Do not invite questions on this slide. The discussion slide already came earlier; this is the parting note. Silence after this slide is the correct response. Let them carry it out.
