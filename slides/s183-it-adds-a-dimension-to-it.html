The frontier question, in its final form: not "what's the better answer" — but "what dimension is missing, such that the answer I want could exist at all?"

The conceptual peak of the whole second half. Keep it spare — let it breathe. The room is holding something disorienting; don't crowd it.

The clean version says data is illumination: the basin was always there, you just couldn't see over the ridge. Comfortable, but it preserves the fantasy that the landscape is fixed and waiting to be discovered.

The deeper truth: a new measurement is not a light on the terrain — it is a new AXIS. The moment you add it, basins exist that could not have existed before. Not hidden — not yet real as a decision. 'Why did the human override' isn't a badly-lit option today; it isn't in the phase space at all. Start capturing it and you don't illuminate an existing option — you CREATE a dimension along which new processes can stabilize.

This is the architect's move, not the feature factory's: the factory optimizes points in a fixed set of dimensions. Adding a dimension was never their job. It's yours — and you're handing them a piece of it.

Close the loop: simulation is dimensional reconnaissance. Conjure the axis synthetically, let the agents move in the enlarged space, see if an attractor forms. If it does, you've found where to point the real sensor. That's what MiroFish is FOR — probing whether an enlarged space contains a basin worth making real.
