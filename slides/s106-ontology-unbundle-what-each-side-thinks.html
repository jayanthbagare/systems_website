THE SEAM · SEMANTIC

Ontology — Unbundle What Each Side Thinks Exists

One word. Three objects.

FINANCE

“vendor”

A payee.

Bank details, credit terms,

payment history.

PROCUREMENT

“vendor”

A supplier.

Catalogue, lead times,

quality record.

CONTRACTS

“vendor”

A counterparty.

Obligations, liabilities,

renewal dates.

The seam chokes on the difference — silently. Each handoff passes an object the other side doesn't quite have. Unbundling the ontology means pulling the tangle apart until each side's entities, relationships, and states are explicit — so the correspondence, and the gaps, become visible.

Ontology, plainly: agreeing on what exists — so the handoff stops dropping meaning.

Say the word 'ontology' once and tether it INSTANTLY to their material — never let it float. 'What the two sides think exists.'

The three-vendors example is the whole argument: one word, three objects. Every developer in the room has fought this in a data-integration project. Let them feel the recognition.

'Unbundle' is the verb — active, not cartographic. Pull the tangle apart until the correspondence AND the gaps are visible. Agents make this newly tractable: they can hold and translate between rich messy definitions that a rigid schema never could.
