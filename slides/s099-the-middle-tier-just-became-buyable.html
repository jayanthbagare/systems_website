For thirty years System 1 meant deterministic rules. Anything needing judgement — however small — had to escalate. There is now something in between.

Rules

Is the tax ID well formed? Is the amount inside tolerance?

No semantics. Microseconds. Free.

Fast typed decision

Is this entity match ambiguous? How severe is this defect? Does this need attention now?

Semantics, no deliberation. One forward pass, tens to hundreds of milliseconds.

Returns an answer AND a calibrated confidence.

Deliberation

Unwind the ownership tree. Reconcile the conflicting MSA clauses. Find the root cause across shifts.

Multi-step, tool-using, investigative.

Seconds to minutes, and priced accordingly.

A System One model takes a state and returns typed answers with probabilities in a single pass — pick an option, place it on a rubric, or answer yes/no with a calibrated number. No text generated, nothing to parse.

Specifications, links and the honest caveats are on the next slide. Treat the tier as real and the vendor numbers as provisional.

The durable teaching is the three-tier picture. The product names are a fast variable — check them the week before each cohort and expect them to change.

What is genuinely new is not speed. It is that the middle tier returns a CALIBRATED CONFIDENCE alongside its answer. A rules engine cannot say 'I am 62% sure'. That number is what makes automatic escalation possible, which is the next slide.

Be honest about the caveats, because a sceptical architect in the room will be. Typed output removes schema errors by construction, not judgement errors — the vendor's own guidance is that zero hallucinations is not zero errors. The open model scores near chance zero-shot and needs fine-tuning and temperature calibration before its confidence means anything. And the published comparisons are third-party runs.

If someone asks whether to adopt it: not for the constraint, not yet. Try it on one high-volume triage step where the cost of a wrong escalation is low.
