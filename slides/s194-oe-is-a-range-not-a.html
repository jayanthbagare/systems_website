V · What changes in your model

ΔOE is a range, not a number.

Systems · Constraints · Agents

1

Estimate at the happy path.

The call count when the agent gets it right first time. This is the number everyone brings to the business case, and it is the optimistic end of a range.

2

Estimate at the worst case the loop permits.

How many calls before something stops it? If the honest answer is ‘nothing stops it’, that is the finding — write it down.

3

Look at the gap between the two.

If they are close, you engineered the loop. If they are far apart, you hoped. The gap is the size of the bet you are asking someone to take.

For your agent, right now: what does it cost if the stopping condition never fires?

Budget per task, compaction triggers, escalation on uncertainty — the harness controls that bound OE are the same ones that make the agent reliable.

Run this as five minutes of pod work, not as a slide. Every pod re-opens their Lab 4 or Lab 5 agent and answers the question in the accent line.

Most pods will find they cannot answer it, because they never set a budget or a hard call cap. That discovery is the deliverable — it is cheap here and expensive in production.

Tie back to the throughput sheet: the workbook takes ΔOE as a single cell. Tell them to write two numbers in it and a note about the gap.

Close the block here. Do not add examples — the pods have their own agent in front of them and that is a better example than any you could give.
