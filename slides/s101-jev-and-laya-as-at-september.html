Jev and Laya, as at September 2026.

Systems · Constraints · Agents

One commercial, one open. Same interface shape — a state, some typed questions, answers with confidence.

Jev — TypeSafe AI

Early access 15 Sep 2026. Hosted API, closed weights. Trained with RLCD — reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions.

Three question types: choice (option from a set), score (ordered rubric), noul (probability a statement is true). Up to 255 options.

Vendor-published: 70–500ms, ~200× faster and ~400× cheaper than an LLM on classification. Third-party runs measured 236–276ms.

typesafe.ai · docs.typesafe.ai

Laya — ConvAI Innovations

Published 19 Sep 2026, four days after Jev. Apache-2.0, open weights, runs on a free Colab T4.

421M English: ModernBERT-large plus a decision head trained from scratch, including an act-versus-escalate head. 322M multilingual variant, 100+ languages.

Author-reported: 32.8ms per question, 7.2ms batched. 0.766 argmax accuracy against Jev's published 0.727 — but Jev scores higher on soft accuracy.

pip install laya · huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya

Before you believe any of the above

Typed output removes schema errors by construction. It does not remove judgement errors — the vendor's own guidance is that zero hallucinations is not zero errors.

Laya scores near chance zero-shot and ships overconfident until its temperature is refit; it reaches 0.081 calibration error only after domain fitting. Its Jev comparisons use published figures, not its own runs of Jev.

Also worth knowing: LangChain ships langchain-typesafe with model-routing and tool-risk-gating middleware. github.com/yibie/awesome-jev tracks open reproductions — mini-jev, kev, jev-local.

This is a photograph-it slide, not a talk-through slide. Give the room fifteen seconds and move on.

The reason it is in the deck at all: a pod that wants to try the middle tier should be able to do it the same week, and the open model makes that a twenty-minute experiment rather than a procurement exercise. pip install laya, a free Colab T4, no API bill.

Say the caveats out loud even though they are written down. The interesting failure mode here is not that the model is wrong — it is that it is confidently wrong in a typed field your code trusts, which is harder to notice than a malformed response. Calibration is not a nicety on this tier; it is the entire safety property.

If a pod asks which to try: Laya, because it is free and local and the point is to learn whether the tier fits their triage step. The commercial decision comes later and only if the experiment works.
