The climb — agent-shaped

The direction is known. Every step can be scored against the same objective.

Gradient is available — exactly where an agent earns its keep.

The plateau — the diagnosis

Returns flatten. More effort, same output.

The temptation is to push harder on the same curve. The balancing loop has already taken over and it will not give the gains back.

The jump — out of distribution

Nobody can score the next curve using this curve's objective function. It is a reframe, not an optimisation.

This is the frontier altitude — and the one thing here you cannot delegate. For now.

This is the sharpest argument in the deck for why the altitude question matters. An agent is a superb climber. It cannot tell you that you are on the wrong curve, because the evidence for that is not expressible in the objective it was given.

In dynamical terms: the climb is motion toward an attractor inside a basin. The jump is a change of basin. You cannot reach it by gradient ascent, because locally every direction out of the basin looks worse.

Say the caveat out loud rather than hiding it — the room will be thinking it. 'For now' is doing real work in that last line. Models are getting better at recombination, and the honest position is that this is a division of labour that holds today, not a law. The test for when it changes: can the system propose an objective nobody gave it, and defend why the old one was the wrong thing to maximise?

Practical read for a pod: if your metric has been flat for three quarters while effort rose, you are on a plateau. Buying a better climber will not help.
